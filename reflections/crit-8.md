# Crit 8 reflection

## What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?

The breakthrough was finding the smallest complete shared loop: join a room, leave an idea, persist it, and let someone else see it. Once that loop existed end to end — a real room code, a real saved suggestion, a second visitor actually finding it there after a refresh — everything else (balanced roles, capacity, the Continue path) could be added as an extension of something that already worked, instead of as pieces of a bigger system I was hoping would eventually connect. Before that, I kept being pulled toward building more of the surrounding structure before the one thing that actually mattered — a visitor leaving a trace that survives — was solid.

## What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?

I used to associate good development with building more: more features, more completeness, more of the planning platform I originally imagined DayDraw could become. This week changed that. A bigger part of the job, I now think, is knowing what not to build, and taking responsibility for the experience of what does get built. DayDraw works this week because it promises exactly what it does — a shared board that remembers your idea — and nothing more. Deciding to leave voting, real-time updates and the rest for later wasn't a shortcut; it was the actual design decision that let the first thing be honest and complete.
