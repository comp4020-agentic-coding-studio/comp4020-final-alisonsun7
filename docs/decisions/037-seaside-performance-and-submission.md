## Record an animal rather than defend a synthesis metric

The rejected `pond-frogs` was an original synthetic illustration. Its peak and
lifecycle checks were valid for those claims, not evidence of a convincing frog.
The replacement takes a natural 3.2-second phrase from Ath's CC0 pond recording,
[Bad Waldliesborn - Brandscherenteich.oga](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Bad_Waldliesborn_-_Brandscherenteich.oga).
Commons's metadata and the downloaded original agree on SHA-1
`e482140e86da6d21fd145fff77bacffd6e1f84f1`. Provenance retains author, exact
source, licence, processing script/hash, output hash and the superseded synthetic
record. The ID, 4.5-second event window and 18-second interval remain unchanged.
No stored source or reply changes meaning.

The derivative is mono with gentle frequency filtering, bounded normalization
and softened edges; pitch, speed and internal timing are retained. The natural
phrase starts at 0.8 seconds and ends at 4.0, followed by silence. Its measured
100ms RMS envelope drives throat motion on the same server/source-time phase.
This is a pond chorus, not two acoustically isolated individual croaks. Original
bubble and whale MP3 bytes remain unchanged. The older synthetic renderer can
no longer overwrite the active frog recording. See the [asset record](../assets/waterlife-sounds.md).

A second real-recording candidate had over-range decoded peaks and was rejected
rather than chosen from its title alone. No human listening verdict is invented
for the selected replacement. An actual private audition remains available for
the author to accept or reject its recognizable quality and relative level.
