# Crit reflection guide

Keep completed honest reflections. New files go at reflections/crit-9.md and reflections/crit-10.md, with crit-8.md repaired only if genuinely incomplete. Do not overwrite an earlier account with hindsight presented as contemporaneous experience. If something is written later, do not falsify date or cutoff status.

For each entry use the two standing prompts: the breakthrough that moved the work forward, and what changed about the developer you want to become. Guidance: 150–300 English words. Wen can provide Chinese notes for editing/translation.

## Fill from a real event

Before: what assumption or difficulty was I working with?
Observation: what actually happened, or what did I inspect?
Change: what did I decide/change and why did that move the work?
Evidence: which actual commit/test/interaction supports this?
Identity: what habit or responsibility do I now want to keep?

## Possible topics to consider, not experiences to fabricate

C8: persistence means more than a page surviving refresh; a real server restart or migration readback changed my understanding; or a clearer role/permission distinction changed the design.
C9: “we emit events” is weaker than another user actually seeing current state; reconnecting revealed a stale-state assumption; or private labels still leave inference in a small group.
C10: logs exposed something UI screenshots hid; pseudonymous actor information helped understand concurrent actions; or an instrumented action had been confused with human attention.

Choose only a topic that actually affected Wen. The reflection should not be a copied test report or a generic paragraph praising AI. Never invent a bug so there is a dramatic breakthrough.

## Ask Wen

“In this week's actual work, what surprised you? What did you choose to check rather than accept? What will you do differently next time?” Use the answers, preserve voice, and label any missing fact instead of supplying a fictional answer.
